The one number to remember

A single batch of 512 costs 110.2 MB in observations alone (s_t + s_next as float32), and collapses to 18.0 MB of latents once encoded — a 6× reduction. That compression is the entire point of the representation-learning stage.

What one training sample is

Both s_t and s_next are 4-frame stacks, and they are offset by exactly one frame — verified by array comparison: next_obs[t][:,:,c] == obs[t][:,:,c+1] holds for all three overlapping channels, and next_obs[t] == obs[t+1] on non-terminal steps.

Why the prediction task is easier than it looks

Because 3 of the 4 input channels are identical between s_t and s_next, the target encoder sees 75 % of what the context encoder saw. A model that simply passed its input through would already score a cosine similarity of 0.9817 against the target. Any evaluation of the predictor has to be read against that copy baseline, not against zero.

The representation is not collapsed — different samples sit near-orthogonal (0.09029 mean pairwise cosine), and the encoder separates games while clustering frames within a game. The predictor beats the copy baseline by 38 % on MSE. The weak spot is action conditioning: swapping in a deliberately wrong action barely moves the prediction (0.9901 cosine to the correct-action prediction), even though the latents clearly contain action information — the inverse head recovers the true action 56.1 % of the time.

Frames, action histograms, pixel statistics and episode counts were read directly from the 15 archives in custom_datasets_small/{train,test} (50,000 transitions each); images are the raw uint8 channels, nearest-neighbour upscaled, no contrast adjustment. Latent measurements come from vjepa_atari_ep10.pt in eval mode, fp32, on a 264-sample stratified batch (numpy seed 0, 24 samples per game); the wrong-action counterfactual uses torch seed 0. Byte figures are computed from shape × itemsize. Sections 01–04 describe the dataset and are identical across checkpoints.
